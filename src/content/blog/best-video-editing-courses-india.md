Search for video editing courses in India and you get a wall of institutes with the same four software logos and no fees. That is no use when the choice is really between a year at a film school, a chain institute near your house, or twelve weekends while you keep your job. So this compares the real options on cost, time and what you leave with, with the source for every figure at the foot of the page.

We run one of the programmes on this list. It is included on the same terms as the rest, including where it is the wrong choice.

## FTII Pune

The best value on this list, and 11 seats for the whole country.

FTII's one-year Post Graduate Certificate Course in Television with specialisation in Video Editing costs ₹99,395 in tuition. With hostel rent, deposits and other charges the total for the year is ₹1,65,847, and some of the deposits are returned. You need a bachelor's degree in any discipline and you sit the two-stage FTII entrance test: a written paper, then an assessment in Pune. There is a ₹50,000 seat acceptance fee, non-refundable but adjusted against the course fee. The 2026 window has closed; classes begin around September.

The three-year MFA in Cinema with specialisation in Editing is the deeper version, about ₹2.81 lakh in tuition over six semesters, also 11 seats.

**Pick FTII if** you can commit a year or three full-time, you can clear the entrance, and a recognised qualification matters to you.

**Do not pick FTII if** you need to keep earning, or you want to be editing paid work within six months. The course is built to make editors, not to place them quickly.

## Whistling Woods International

Whistling Woods has no standalone editing course. Editing is a specialisation inside the two-year MFA in Filmmaking, and the fee reflects the whole film school: ₹8.5 lakh in tuition plus ₹17.5 lakh in infrastructure, equipment and project fees, plus a ₹50,000 deposit, which the institute's fee policy rounds to ₹26 lakh. GST at 18% is on top, as is an Adobe Creative Cloud licence you must buy yourself. You need a degree. There are 150 seats across all the MFA specialisations, not 150 for editing.

**Pick Whistling Woods if** you want to edit Hindi film and streaming, you want the network from day one, and ₹30 lakh all-in is genuinely affordable.

**Do not pick Whistling Woods if** it means a loan. Editing in India starts at ₹15,000 to ₹35,000 a month for the first two years. We wrote up [what editors actually earn](/blog/video-editor-earnings-india) separately, and those ranges do not service that debt.

## SRFTI Kolkata

The government option most people forget. SRFTI's MFA in Cinema with specialisation in Film Editing runs a one-year bridge programme for people who did not study film, then two years of MFA. Tuition is ₹47,861 a semester, plus an ₹8,493 admission fee, a ₹71,038 security deposit and hostel at ₹25,092 a semester. Twelve seats. Admission is by a two-stage test, and the 2026 cycle closed in February.

**Pick SRFTI if** government fees and a full-time course suit you, and Kolkata and the eastern industries are where you intend to work.

**Do not pick SRFTI if** three years is more than you have.

## Chain institutes

Arena Animation and MAAC have centres in most Indian cities, and for many people they are the only classroom within reach. That is their real advantage. The honest weakness is that neither brand publishes a fee on its official site, so you are quoted a price by the centre.

Arena's Delhi and Noida centres list video editing at ₹1 lakh to ₹5 lakh depending on track, from six months to three years, two hours a day, six days a week, with a 10+2 as the entry requirement and Premiere Pro, After Effects and Audition as the core software. MAAC's Kolkata academy lists a 272-hour video production programme and says outright that the fee varies by city. One MAAC franchisee's own page says a high fee does not guarantee placement and tells you to ask for employer names and batch outcomes. That is good advice for every institute on this page.

**Pick a chain institute if** you need a physical classroom, you are under 20 and a structured daily timetable is what will make you turn up.

**Do not pick one without** the fee in writing, the names of the last batch's employers, and a look at what the last batch actually cut.

## Live online cohorts

The category we are in. LevelUp's Video Editing Academy runs twelve weeks of live sessions on weekends, with recordings kept for life, at ₹40,000, with EMI and no-cost EMI available. It teaches Premiere Pro, After Effects, DaVinci Resolve and the current generative tools, and the page says to expect five to six hours a week. There is no eligibility requirement. The application fee is refundable, though the page does not state the amount, and it does not state the batch size. Both are things we should fix on that page.

**Pick a live cohort if** you have a job, you already know what a timeline is, and what you lack is someone experienced looking at your cuts every week and telling you what is wrong.

**Do not pick one if** you want a recognised qualification, or you have never opened editing software. Learn the buttons on a recorded course first; it is cheaper.

## Recorded courses

Udemy's Adobe Premiere Pro Masterclass has over half a million students and a 4.6 rating from more than 46,000 of them. It was ₹469 on sale on the day we checked, against a list price of ₹3,289, and Udemy runs sales most weeks. Internshala's six-week Video Editing Course is ₹1,110, recorded, in English and Hindi, with an NSDC certificate at the end.

**Pick a recorded course if** you want to learn the software. That is what these teach, and they teach it well for very little money.

**Do not pick one as** your only training if you intend to do this for a living. Nobody watches your work, nobody tells you the cut is wrong, and every edit you make is a decision you have not been taught to make.

## So which one

Answer two questions. **Can you stop earning for a year?** And **have you already learnt the software?**

If you can stop earning and can clear the entrance, FTII is the most training per rupee in the country, and SRFTI is the same idea in Kolkata. If money is no constraint, Whistling Woods buys the Mumbai network at a price no other editing course comes close to. If you cannot stop earning and you do not yet know the software, start with a recorded course for under ₹1,500 and find out whether you like the work. If you cannot stop earning and you do know the software, a live cohort is the only category that puts an experienced editor in front of your cuts every week without leaving your job. That is what the ₹40,000 buys, not the software.

## Common questions

**Does the fee include hostel and GST?**
At FTII and SRFTI, no: hostel rent and deposits are extra, and some deposits are refundable. Whistling Woods adds 18% GST and a security deposit to its ₹26 lakh. Chain institutes quote per centre. Add it all up before comparing.

**Is placement guaranteed?**
Nowhere on this page. Chain institutes offer placement assistance, which is not a guarantee. LevelUp offers placement support, which is not one either. Ask any institute for the names of the companies that hired its last batch.

**Which software should I learn?**
Premiere Pro is on every course here and is what most Indian agencies and creators use. DaVinci Resolve is free and growing fast in film. After Effects is where motion work happens. Learn Premiere first, whatever course you choose.

**Can I learn video editing online for free?**
The software, yes, and well. What you cannot get for free is someone experienced watching your decisions. That is the whole difference between the top and bottom of this table.

**Do I need a degree?**
For FTII, SRFTI and Whistling Woods, yes. Arena takes a 10+2. Live cohorts and recorded courses need nothing.
