Most lists of screenwriting courses in India copy fees off a brochure and rank the schools by reputation. That does not help when the real question is whether you can stop working for two years, or need something that fits around a job. So this compares the options on cost, time and what you actually get, with the source for every figure at the foot of the page.

We run two of the programmes on this list. They are included on the same terms as the rest, including where they are the wrong choice.

Verify against the current prospectus before you apply, because every one of these institutes revises fees between cycles.

## FTII Pune

The best value on this list by a wide margin, and the hardest to get into.

FTII runs two long courses that teach screenwriting. The two-year MFA in Cinema with specialisation in Screenwriting takes 16 students a year. Tuition is ₹46,885 in the first semester and ₹46,884 in each of the next three, about ₹1.88 lakh over the course. On top of that the first semester carries ₹62,689 in hostel rent, deposits and other charges, and later semesters about ₹19,408 each. Several of the deposits come back when you leave. There is also a non-refundable seat acceptance fee of ₹50,000, adjusted against the course fee.

The three-year MFA in Direction and Screenplay Writing takes 11 students and costs about ₹2.81 lakh in tuition over six semesters. Both need a bachelor's degree in any discipline and the two-stage FTII entrance test: a written paper, then an assessment at the institute in Pune. The 2026 window has closed. Classes begin around September.

FTII also runs a very different thing: a Foundation Course in Screenplay Writing from its writers' academy, online, on ten weekends, for ₹20,000. It takes 24 people, needs only a 10th pass and an age of 15 or more, and admits on a first-come basis. Batches run on Saturdays and Sundays. The fee is non-refundable. A sibling Basic Course in the Art of Screenwriting runs on the same pattern for ₹22,000.

**Pick the FTII MFA if** you can commit two or three years full-time, you can clear a competitive entrance, and a recognised qualification matters to you.

**Pick the FTII weekend course if** you want FTII's teaching and a certificate for the price of a phone, and you are honest that ten weekends will not make you a professional.

**Do not pick FTII if** you need to keep earning. The MFA has no part-time route.

## Whistling Woods International

The most expensive option here and the most connected to Mumbai.

The one-year Diploma in Screenwriting is ₹5.5 lakh including a ₹50,000 security deposit, with 18% GST on top. It takes 30 students, needs only a 10+2, and prefers graduates and working professionals. Admission is through Whistling Woods' own entrance test, a creative test, a statement of purpose and an interview. The page's own instalment schedule adds up to ₹6 lakh rather than ₹5.5 lakh, so ask admissions which figure you will pay.

The two-year MA in Screenwriting is ₹8.5 lakh, or ₹9.5 lakh with the security deposit, plus GST. It needs a degree and takes 30.

**Pick Whistling Woods if** you intend to write for Hindi film and streaming, you want to be inside that network from the first week, and the fee is genuinely affordable to your family.

**Do not pick Whistling Woods if** the fee would need a loan you cannot service on a writer's early income. Screenwriting in India pays late, and ₹6 lakh of debt against that is a hard start.

## SRFTI Kolkata

The government option most people outside the east forget.

SRFTI's MFA in Cinema with specialisation in Direction and Screenplay Writing runs as a one-year bridge programme followed by a two-year MFA. The bridge year exists for students who did not study film before, which describes most applicants. Tuition is ₹47,861 a semester, with a one-time admission fee of ₹8,493, a ₹71,038 security deposit and hostel at ₹25,092 a semester. Admission is by a two-stage test, and the 2026 cycle closed in February. SRFTI's pages give 12 seats but do not say whether that is per specialisation or for the whole MFA.

**Pick SRFTI if** you want government fees and a full-time course, and Kolkata and the eastern industries are where you intend to work.

**Do not pick SRFTI if** three years is more than you have, or Mumbai is the only industry you care about.

## Online diplomas

Two Indian options publish a fee and a schedule.

Mindscreen Film Institute in Chennai runs a six-month screenwriting certificate online, on weekends over Zoom, for ₹65,000 plus GST with a ₹1,000 registration fee. Applicants need to be 18 or over and write a one-page story idea and a statement of purpose. Its faculty and contacts are in the Tamil industry, which is the reason to choose it.

AAFT Online runs a six-month Diploma in Screenwriting for ₹75,000, with live classes and recordings available for a year.

**Pick an online diploma if** you need structure and a deadline more than you need a room, and you cannot travel.

**Do not pick one if** what you are missing is people who will read your pages every week. Recorded lectures do not do that.

## Short live programmes and retreats

The category we are in.

LevelUp's Screenwriting and Storytelling Program runs eight weeks of live sessions every Sunday, with recordings kept for life. There is no eligibility requirement, though the page says it accepts a small share of applicants. The fee is not listed on the page; you apply and are told. That is a weakness of our own page, and we say so here rather than pretend otherwise.

The Forge Writing retreat is six days in one place with about 20 writers and two or three mentors. The fee changes with the edition, the season and the country, so check the current edition on the Forge page. The point of it is the draft you leave with, not a certificate.

**Pick a short programme if** you have a job, you have been writing on your own, and what you lack is feedback and a deadline.

**Do not pick one if** you want a qualification, or you are hoping a certificate will get you a writing job. It will not. What gets you hired is a script someone read and liked.

## So which one

Answer one question first: **can you stop earning for two years?**

If yes, and you can clear the entrance, FTII gives you the most training per rupee of anything in the country, and SRFTI is the same idea at the other end of it. If yes and the money is not a constraint, Whistling Woods buys the Mumbai network. If no, the honest options are FTII's ten-weekend course for ₹20,000, an online diploma, or a short live programme, and the trade is that you get less depth than a two-year course, because two years is longer than eight weeks.

If you are not sure you want this at all, do not spend ₹6 lakh finding out. Write a short script first. Several of the cheaper options exist for exactly that.

## Common questions

**Does the fee include hostel and GST?**
Rarely. FTII and SRFTI list tuition separately from hostel rent and deposits, and some deposits are refundable. Whistling Woods adds 18% GST and a security deposit to its stated fee. Add these up before comparing.

**Can I do a serious screenwriting course part-time in India?**
Yes. FTII's writers' academy runs weekend courses online, Mindscreen and AAFT run online diplomas, and LevelUp runs live Sunday sessions. None of them is a substitute for a two-year MFA, and none of them pretends to be.

**Is there an entrance test?**
For the long courses, yes: FTII, SRFTI and Whistling Woods all test. Most short courses admit on a first-come basis or on a short application.

**Do I need a degree?**
For FTII and SRFTI MFAs and the Whistling Woods MA, yes. The Whistling Woods diploma takes a 10+2. Short courses mostly need a 10th pass or nothing at all.

**Is the fee refundable if I drop out?**
FTII's short courses say the fee is non-refundable, non-transferable and non-adjustable. Mindscreen keeps its admission fee. Read the refund line before you pay any of them, including ours.
