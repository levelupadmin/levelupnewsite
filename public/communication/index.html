Curriculum

Twelve weeks, four acts.

The full syllabus, in public. Every week has one live session, two practice labs and one assignment taken from your own work.

Act IThink clearlyWeeks 1 to 3

Why capable professionals go quiet in the rooms that matter, and the fear mechanics behind it. You record a two minute introduction as your baseline and sit for a one to one call that maps the three rooms you want changed by week twelve.

Assignment · the baseline recordingAlso · goal mapping call

Structured thinking as the root skill. Frameworks that turn a messy thought into a clear answer in sixty seconds, and hold that answer when someone interrupts.

Assignment · restructure a real work updateYou keep · your structure card

Pace, pause, posture and eye contact, plus executive presence on the video calls where most of your career now happens. You re-record your week one introduction and watch the difference.

Assignment · the re-recordYou keep · your locked camera setup
Act IISpeak so it landsWeeks 4 to 7

The psychology of storytelling applied to business, and how to turn your own experience into story capital you can use in a pitch, a panel or a one to one.

Assignment · five stories, written and recordedYou keep · your story bank

Claim, evidence, concession, ask. Influence without authority, and how to pre-wire a room before you walk into it. You pitch a real idea from your workplace while your group plays the sceptical panel.

Assignment · a real proposal, pitchedRule · it has to be genuinely live at work

The daily arena. Status updates that build your standing, speaking up early, running meetings people thank you for, and recovering after an interruption. Plus written and async communication that reads senior.

Assignment · midpoint diagnosticAlso · your second score, in a one to one

The number is not the point, the change is. Slide narration, one chart one message, and hostile questions handled with composure. The questions come from mentors, not peers, because peers go easy.

Assignment · three slides from your real data
Act IIILead the hard roomsWeeks 8 to 10

Feedback that lands, and feedback received without armour. Disagreeing upward safely, negotiating, and staying composed when the stakes are visible to everyone. A one to one checkpoint locks your final talk topic.

Assignment · the conversation you have been avoiding

Interview narratives built off the story bank from week four. Appraisal and salary conversations argued on evidence instead of hope. A twenty minute mock panel, recorded and graded.

Assignment · your evidence doc, written and spoken

Communicating vision and expectations. Delegation that empowers instead of dumps. Leading without authority, and running the rooms you used to sit silent in.

Assignment · a vision brief for a real teamYou keep · your room playbook
Act IVThe signature talkWeeks 11 to 12

Openings that earn attention and closings that stick. Two separate rehearsal passes, a table read where the notes are only about structure, then a dress rehearsal where the notes are only about delivery. Mixing the two is why most rehearsals fail.

Assignment · final script plus two rehearsal recordings

Your five to seven minute talk on a real topic from your own work, delivered to a panel and your cohort. Your third and final score on the same scorecard.

The change

The leader you become.

Here is what that change looks like in practice.

Say it in thirty seconds

Answer first, evidence after. The clarity to articulate a position in one breath instead of a four minute run up while the room drifts.

Lead the meeting, not just attend it

Speak up early, carry executive presence on the global call and in the room, and recover after an interruption instead of losing the thread.

Get a yes without the title

Build the case, pre-wire your stakeholders, and hold your position with conviction when the pushback starts.

Handle the hard question

Composure in high-stakes questioning, and the judgement to say I don’t know without losing credibility.

Have the difficult conversation

Give feedback that lands, disagree upward without losing trust, and finish the conversation you have been rehearsing for a month.

Negotiate your own case

Your appraisal, your next offer, and the resources your team needs, argued on evidence and negotiated instead of hoped for.
