The change

The leader you become.

Here’s what that looks like at work.

Get to the point in 30 seconds

Answer first, then explain. No long build-up while people lose interest.

Speak up in meetings

Share your idea early and clearly, so it stays yours.

Present with confidence

To your team, to senior leaders, or on a video call.

Handle tough questions

Stay calm, think on your feet, and say “I don’t know” without losing respect.

Have the difficult conversation

Give feedback, disagree with your boss, and still keep the relationship.

Make your own case

In appraisals and interviews, show the value of your work with facts.

The skills

What you’ll work on.

Six skills, each taught live, practised every week, and used in your real work.

01

Fast, structured thinking

Get the point out first and the support after. Most communication problems are unstructured thinking in disguise.

02

Communication and public speaking

Say it once, clearly, to four people or four hundred, without the long build-up.

03

Executive presence

How you sound and how you carry yourself decide whether people believe what you say.

04

Storytelling and presentations

Turn your own experience into stories that land, and present data so people remember the point, not just the number.

05

Getting a yes from stakeholders who don’t report to you, and holding it when they push back.

06

Leadership and difficult conversations

The conversation you have been avoiding, the disagreement with your boss, and the meeting that ends in a decision.

Curriculum

Twelve weeks, four parts.

The full syllabus, in public. Every week has live weekend sessions, two practice labs and one assignment from your own work.

Part 1Think clearlyWeeks 1 to 3

Why capable professionals go quiet in the meetings that matter, and what’s really behind the fear. You record a two-minute introduction as your starting point and have a one-to-one call that maps the three situations at work you most want to handle better.

Assignment · your starting-point videoAlso · goal-mapping call

Structured thinking as the root skill. Simple frameworks that turn a messy thought into a clear answer in sixty seconds, and help you hold it when someone interrupts.

Assignment · restructure a real work updateYou keep · your structure card

Pace, pause, posture and eye contact, plus presence on the video calls where most of your career now happens. You re-record your week one introduction and see the difference.

Assignment · the re-recordYou keep · your camera setup
Part 2Speak so it landsWeeks 4 to 7

Storytelling applied to business, and how to turn your own experience into stories you can use in a pitch, a panel or a one-to-one.

Assignment · five stories, written and recordedYou keep · your story bank

Claim, evidence, concession, ask. Influence without authority, and how to get people on your side before the meeting starts. You pitch a real idea from your workplace in front of a sceptical panel of mentors.

Assignment · a real proposal, pitchedRule · it has to be genuinely live at work

Where it happens every day. Status updates that build your standing, speaking up early, running meetings people thank you for, and recovering after an interruption. Plus emails and messages that read senior.

Assignment · midpoint checkAlso · your second progress review, one to one

The number is not the point, the change is. Presenting slides, one chart one message, and handling tough questions calmly. The tough questions come from mentors.

Assignment · three slides from your real data
Part 3Lead the hard conversationsWeeks 8 to 10

Giving feedback that lands, and taking it without getting defensive. Disagreeing with your boss safely, negotiating, and staying calm when everyone is watching. A one-to-one check-in locks your final talk topic.

Assignment · the conversation you have been avoiding

Interview answers built from your week 4 stories. Appraisal and salary conversations argued on evidence instead of hope. A twenty-minute mock panel, recorded, with feedback.

Assignment · your evidence doc, written and spoken

Communicating vision and expectations. Delegation that empowers instead of dumps. Leading without authority, and running the meetings you used to sit silent in.

Assignment · a vision brief for a real teamYou keep · your leadership playbook
Part 4Your signature talkWeeks 11 to 12

Openings that get attention and closings that stick. Two separate rehearsals: one where the notes are only about structure, then one where the notes are only about delivery. Mixing the two is why most rehearsals fail.

Assignment · final script plus two rehearsal recordings

Your five to seven minute talk on a real topic from your own work, delivered to a panel and professionally filmed. Your final progress review, next to your week 1 video.
